Play first

This page is for anyone, of any age, who wants to see how learning machines work before reading a line of code. Each step is something to try with your mouse or finger. Nothing here is a video: every network on this page is really learning, right now, in your browser.

1. Pictures are numbers

Every picture on a screen is a grid of numbers. Move over the photo and look at the numbers hiding under it. Dark is a small number, bright is a big one.

2. Teach a computer something new

Make up two kinds of drawing. Draw a few of each and tell the computer which is which. Then press train, draw a new one, and see if it guesses right. You just taught a machine!

3. Draw a line between two colors

Pick yours, click to put orange and blue dots anywhere you like, and press train. Can the computer draw a border between your colors? Try to make one it cannot do with a straight line, then give it hidden units.

4. Beat the matchboxes

A pile of matchboxes full of beads can learn to play noughts and crosses. Play against it, then let it practice: it learns from nothing but winning and losing.

5. Learning by guessing

A computer can learn just by guessing and keeping the guesses that work better. Watch it climb toward the brightest peak.

6. Coin flips make a bell

Each ball flips a coin at every pin. One ball goes anywhere, but a thousand balls make the same shape every time.

7. Tiny rules, big patterns

Each square looks only at its eight neighbors, again and again. Out of that come walkers, waves and stripes. Click to poke the world, and try changing the rule.

8. What is the computer looking at?

Cover part of a handwritten number with a black square and watch the computer change its mind. Press map to see which parts matter most.

9. A real network's eyes

A famous network learned these 64 tiny patterns by looking at a million photos. Click one to see where it finds that pattern in a picture of a cat.

10. Look inside a famous network

Pick a photo and watch it flow through AlexNet, layer by layer. Click a layer to see everything it computed, and see the strange pictures its deepest units dream of.

11. Squeeze a picture

Draw a number. The computer squeezes your drawing into just a few numbers, then tries to draw it again from those numbers alone. Slide k down: how few numbers can it get away with? Then click on the map to make digits nobody drew.

12. A machine that imagines

Draw any hills you like. Two little networks play a game: one makes numbers, the other tries to catch the fakes. Watch the orange line learn to copy your drawing.

When you want to know how these work, and how to build them yourself, the hands-on chapters start with the numbers inside a picture.
